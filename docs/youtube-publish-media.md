# YouTube media selection and Content ID safety

Media published to YouTube must use audio and visual assets that protect the
channel from copyright and Content ID claims. Permissive stock licenses do not
guarantee safety against automated Content ID matches.

## Content ID rules for YouTube uploads

Every video or clip uploaded to YouTube must follow these asset rules:

1. **Clear audio and visual sources before publishing**: background music
   (BGM), sound effects (SFX), and still images must come from YouTube Audio
   Library or sources the operator explicitly verified as Content ID-free for
   YouTube. Keep verification records outside the public repository when they
   contain account details.
2. **Stock-site licenses do not guarantee Content ID clearance**: a permissive
   license granting commercial or free use on a stock site does not prevent
   automated YouTube claims. Do not use Pixabay or similar third-party stock
   audio as default soundtrack material for YouTube uploads.
3. **Prefer native or original audio**: use YouTube Audio Library tracks,
   original recorded audio, or silence instead of third-party stock audio.
4. **Clear visual assets before export**: use self-made screen recordings,
   frames from owned media, or visual libraries cleared for YouTube.
   Unverified stock images risk matching Content ID.

## Operator and agent video lane checklist

Verify each step before uploading or handing video off for human upload:

- [ ] Confirm the soundtrack source is YouTube Audio Library or documented
  as Content ID-free for YouTube.
- [ ] Confirm no Pixabay or generic stock audio remains in the edit based on
  a stock license alone.
- [ ] Confirm sound effects and still images follow the same verified clearing
  rule.
- [ ] If replacing audio after a claim: swap the track to YouTube Audio
  Library or original audio, re-export, and update the video in YouTube Studio.
  Do not dispute the claim on the stock-site license alone.
